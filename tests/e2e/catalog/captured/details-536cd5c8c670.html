Bilbo Baggins, a gentle hobbit who loves the comforts of home, reluctantly joins a company of dwarves on a journey to recover plundered gold from a fierce dragon. It’s a tale of high adventure and astonishing courage, and a magical prelude to the Lord of the Rings trilogy.

In 1979, the Mind’s Eye produced this radio series of The Hobbit, adapted and directed by Bob Lewis. Radio drama was a rarity in the United States at that time, and this production was popular at the time of its broadcast on National Public Radio. Mind’s Eye would reuse most of the cast for their radio series of The Lord of the Rings of the same year (not to be confused with the superior BBC radio production produced in 1981).

The music featured in the radio drama were pulled from production music produced by Major Records. Primarily Featuring composer Roger Roger but also contains works from George Chase and Sam Trust. They would use the same music library for their adaptation of Lord of The Rings, some pieces being shared between the two.

This is not my rip. I created the cover art based on an existing paperback cover, since no decent album art existed, and edited the files into a chapterized m4b. I also removed the part credits that I believe appeared at the start and end of each tape/disc, so it now runs as a seamless omnibus edition, with no interruptions throughout. It is instead broken into the chapters from the book.

Is it any good? I don’t know - I have only skimmed it! This was more in the spirit of preservation for Tolkien completists. In my opinion, the American accents are a little jarring in Middle Earth, and some of the name pronunciations will sound off to anyone used to Peter Jackson’s movies. Also, the performers here apparently recorded all their lines independently, unlike similar BBC audio dramas that gathered all the performers in one room to perform together. The end result is that the performances are just not as good. Either way, it’s an interesting curiosity, and offers an alternative to the BBC Hobbit audio drama produced in 1969, only a year after the Hobbit was published.
